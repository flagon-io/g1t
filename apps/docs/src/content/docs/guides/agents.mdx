**An agent is a colleague you hire into a role.** Every workspace starts
with one agent, `@g1t`, the orchestrator. Every other agent is one you
hire, usually from a template in a click: for example, Margo from the **QA
Engineer** template, Sam from **Support Specialist**, David from **Sales
Operations**. Each has a name, a handle
you mention it by, a title, a list of things it answers for, and a voice
of its own. It is on [teams](/guides/teams/) the way a person is, and sits
in the member list next to the people. You DM it,
invite it to channels, and it answers where you asked. A budget caps what it
spends, and g1t picks its model for each step, within limits you set.

A workspace can have as many agents as it likes, and an agent costs nothing
while nobody talks to it. Hire one from each template, add each to its
team, and a workspace's org chart can read like a real company's:

| Team | Hired from the template | As |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering | Otto | Software Engineer |
| QA | Margo | QA Engineer |
| Docs | Inky | Technical Writer |
| Product | Dot | Product Manager |
| Customer Support | Sam | Support Specialist |
| Sales | David | Sales Operations |
| Operations | Bruno | Operations Engineer |

None of these come with a workspace: they are what you might hire. Above
them all is `@g1t`, who comes with every workspace and knows everyone you
hire.

## g1t, the orchestrator

Every workspace has `@g1t` from the start. You don't create it and can't
archive it. It is pinned at the top of the Agents list, marked
**Orchestrator**, and it is the one to talk to when you don't know who
should do something.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **In every workspace** | On every surface: Chat, issues and pull requests, Notifications and MCP. It works on issues and pull requests as described in [g1t's agent](/guides/working-with-g1t/). |
| **Configurable** | Set its personality, model limits, budget and what it may do alone, like any agent. Its job is fixed, and you can add instructions to it. |
| **Knows the team** | Every agent you hire: its handle, title, the teams it is on, responsibilities, whether it is idle, working, paused or out of budget, and its spend this month against its monthly cap. |
| **Delegates** | When a specialist's role fits, it [hands the work off](#hand-off) with a brief: here, if the specialist is in the conversation, or in a group message with you and them. At most two hand-offs per message. |
| **Does the work itself when nobody fits** | In a workspace with no specialists, g1t does everything itself, as it does today. |
| **Reports** | Answers "what's everyone working on?" from what it knows of the team. A daily or weekly summary of what the team's agents did is <Soon /> |

`g1t` is never another agent's handle.

## Hire an agent

Owners create the workspace's agents, because such an agent spends the
workspace's money and speaks in its name. Any member can create a
[personal agent](#personal-agents) of their own, unless an owner turned
that off. Every member sees the workspace's agents and talks to them.

### Describe it

<Steps>

1. Open **Agents** in the rail, then choose **New agent**, or go to
   `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/agents/new`.
2. In **What should this agent do?**, describe the job in a sentence or a
   paragraph: what it does, for whom, and what good looks like. Owners
   choose **The workspace** or **Just me**; anyone else's agent is their
   personal one.
3. Choose **Draft it**. g1t drafts the whole agent with one quick model
   call, charged to you: a name (choose **Another name** to shuffle), a
   handle, a [face](#its-face) of its own, a title, its job, its
   responsibilities and its personality, which
   [skills](/guides/agent-skills/) it keeps on, the integrations it would
   use, routines that would suit it, its model limits and its budget.
4. Change anything in the card. Only the name and the job are required.
5. **Try it** in the test chat beside the card: ask what it would get
   asked. It answers from its job and voice exactly as it will in a direct
   message, without tools or memory, since it doesn't exist yet. Each answer
   is charged to you, and nothing is saved.
6. Under **Add to teams**, choose any of the workspace's teams it should
   join, as you would add a person. You see the teams you may add agents
   to: every team for an owner, and the teams you maintain. A personal
   agent is on no team.
7. Choose **Create**. Or choose **Edit all fields** for the full form,
   with the draft filled in.

</Steps>

Nothing is saved until you create it. Leaving the page drops the draft
and the test chat.

| In the draft | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Skills** | The [foundational skills](/guides/agent-skills/) its job needs are marked **Suggested** and on; the rest are off. Turn any on or off. |
| **What it needs connected** | Integrations its job would use, with why. Owners choose **Connect**; anyone else **Ask an owner**, which opens the integration's [Marketplace](/guides/marketplace/) page. **Soon** means nobody can connect it yet. The agent works without them and says so when asked for something that needs one. |
| **Routines that would suit it** | Set them up on its **Routines** tab once it exists. Personal agents can't run routines yet. |
| **Models and budget** | A personal agent starts with **$20 a month** and **$2 a session**. A workspace agent starts with the workspace's budget for a new agent. |

Drafting and trying are each one fast-tier model call, counted against
your own [budget](/guides/agent-budgets/) and the workspace's agent budget.
They show on Spend as **Drafting new agents**. One person can draft and try
60 times an hour.

### Start from a role or every field

Under the box, **or start from a role** lists the
[role templates](#role-templates): choose one to start from its full form.
**Edit all fields** starts from a blank form. Or open **Templates** in the
Agents sidebar (`g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/agents/templates`), read one in full
and choose **Start from this**.

In the form, check its **title** and **responsibilities** (see
[title and responsibilities](#title-and-responsibilities)), choose the
teams under **Add to teams**, check its **personality**, and its
[model limits](#model-routing) and [budget](#budgets), then choose
**Create agent**.

### Its hello

The agent appears on the Agents page as **Idle**, and a direct message
between you and it opens in Chat, where it says hello in its own voice: who
it is, what it will do, and a thing or two to ask it first.
That hello is a reply like any other, charged to its budget; when the
workspace has no model it can use, it sends a short fixed hello instead.
Invite a workspace agent to the channels its team works in.

### Example: hire Margo from the QA template

<Steps>

1. On **New agent**, pick **QA Engineer** under the roles. The name is
   *Margo*, the title is *QA Engineer*, the personality is *Crisp*, and her
   models never go below Standard, because review must be careful.
2. Under **Add to teams**, choose your **QA** team.
3. Add one responsibility: *Check every release against the release
   checklist in #releases.*
4. Set a monthly budget of **$40** and a per-session cap of **$5**.
5. Choose **Create agent**, then invite `@margo` to `#web` and `#releases`.

</Steps>

Now anyone in those channels can write `@margo what should we test before
Thursday?` and get an answer in the thread.

## Change it in words

On an agent's **Profile**, **Tell *name* what to change** takes a change in
your own words, such as *Answer in Spanish, and keep replies under five
sentences*. Choose **Draft the change**: g1t shows each field it would
change, before and after, with the job as a line-by-line diff. Choose
**Save as version N** to save it as a new [version](#the-agents-page), or
**Discard**. Drafting the change is one quick model call, charged to you.
If someone saved the agent since you drafted the change, draft it again.

Owners change the workspace's agents this way; a member changes their own
personal agent.

## Personal agents

A personal agent is a member's own.

| | A workspace agent | A personal agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who creates it** | Owners | Any member, unless owners turned it off. Owners can make one for themselves. |
| **Who talks to it** | Everyone in the workspace, in channels and direct messages | Only its member, in their direct message with it. It can't be invited to a channel or a group message, isn't offered as a mention, and other agents never consult it or hand it work. |
| **Who changes it** | Owners | Its member. Owners can archive it or [promote](#promote-a-personal-agent) it. |
| **Who sees it** | Everyone | Its member and the workspace's owners |
| **Who pays** | The workspace's agent budget | Its member's own [budget](/guides/agent-budgets/), inside the workspace's agent budget |
| **Starting budget** | The workspace's budget for a new agent | $20 a month and $2 a session |
| **Teams and routines** | Yes | No team. Routines <Soon /> |

Your personal agents are under **Yours** in the Agents sidebar, and on
their pages beside a **Yours** badge. Owners see members' personal agents
under **Members' personal**, folded. A member keeps at most 10 personal
agents in a workspace.

### Turn personal agents off

Members can create personal agents unless an owner turns it off:

<Steps>

1. Open **Agents** in the rail, then **Overview**.
2. Under **Personal agents**, switch off **Members can create personal
   agents**.

</Steps>

Off, only owners create agents. Personal agents members already have keep
working. The change is in the workspace's audit log.

### Promote a personal agent

An owner can make a member's personal agent a workspace agent that
everyone can talk to:

<Steps>

1. Open the personal agent's **Profile**. Owners find members' agents under
   **Members' personal** in the Agents sidebar, or under **Personal agents**
   on the Overview.
2. Choose **Promote to a workspace agent…**, then **Promote *name***.

</Steps>

A workspace agent with the same handle, job and every version so far takes
its place, with one more version saying who promoted it. The personal agent
is archived, and what it remembered from its member's direct messages stays
with it: owners can't read a member's memory, so none is carried over.
Promotions, like creating, changing and archiving agents, are in the
workspace's audit log.

## Role templates

Templates are starting points g1t provides, the way you would set up an
assistant's instructions yourself: each is a role you configure, rename and
change. A template names a role, never a team: you add the agent to
teams yourself. Each starts with a fun name (and more to shuffle through),
a title, broad responsibilities, a voice, sensible model limits, and a
subagent or two for its own work. Nothing is installed: once
you start an agent from a template, the agent is yours, and the template
never changes under it.

**Templates** in the Agents sidebar, at `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/agents/templates`,
lists them all, with the agents in your workspace that started from each.
Choose one to read all of it: what it's responsible for, the helpers it
works with, the instructions it starts from, and what you configure when
you start one. Every member can read them; owners start agents from them.

| Template | Suggested name | Title | Answers for | Voice | Models |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering | Otto, or Pixel, Bolt, Tinker… | Software Engineer | Implementing issues; fixing bugs with a test that proves the fix; healthy dependencies and builds | Crisp | Auto |
| QA | Margo, or Wren, Hawk, Edna… | QA Engineer | Reviewing pull requests for risk and test coverage; test plans; flaky checks; reproducing bug reports | Crisp | Never below Standard |
| Operations | Bruno, or Skipper, Patch, Scout… | Operations Engineer | Cutting releases; watching deploys; first response to incidents; postmortems | Terse operator | Never below Standard |
| Docs | Inky, or Quill, Folio, Rosie… | Technical Writer | Docs that are true after every change; decisions turned into pages; release notes | Friendly | Never above Standard |
| Product | Dot, or Clover, Mabel, Penny… | Product Manager | Requests turned into intake; triage and duplicates; roadmap notes; telling people when it ships | Friendly | Never above Standard |
| Customer Support | Sam, or Robin, Jamie, Sunny… | Support Specialist | Product questions from the support team; customer bugs turned into intake; word when a fix ships | Friendly | Never above Standard |
| Sales | David | Sales Operations | Account summaries, the voice-of-the-customer digest, notes before calls | Crisp | Never above Standard |

Planning isn't a role: it is `@g1t`'s own job.

Model limits follow the work. Careful review never runs on the fast tier;
high-volume intake and summaries never run on the most capable one. Change
either if your team works differently.

## Title and responsibilities

A role is broad on purpose. You hire Margo into QA, not into "review pull
request #418".

| Field | What it is | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | What people see: *Margo*. | Up to 64 characters. |
| Handle | How it is mentioned: `@margo`. | 2 to 32 lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens, starting and ending with a letter or digit. Unique in the workspace. Never `g1t` or another reserved name. |
| Title | *QA Engineer*. | Up to 60 characters. |
| Role | The line lists show: *QA Engineer*. Its title unless you write it. | Up to 120 characters. |
| Responsibilities | What it answers for, one per line. | 2 to 8, each up to 160 characters, or none yet. |
| Job | Its instructions: how it works and what good looks like. | Up to 8,000 characters. |
| Personality | A preset, plus free text that refines the voice. | Free text up to 1,000 characters. |
| Subagents | Help it keeps for its own work. See [subagents](#subagents). | Up to 8. They run inside its sessions. |
| Required reading | Spaces in [Artifacts](/guides/artifacts/) it checks first, every time it answers or works. It still reads only what the person it works for, and everyone reading its answer, can open. | Up to 10 spaces. |
| Models | A floor, a ceiling and the providers it may use. | See [model routing](#model-routing). |
| Budget | A monthly cap, a daily cap and a cap per session. | Each optional, up to $100,000. |
| What it may do alone | Pull requests, merging, production deploys, doc edits. | See [what it may do alone](#what-it-may-do-alone). |
| Capacity | How many sessions it works on at once. | 1 to 10. Default 3. |

Every change to an agent is saved as a new **version**, so what it ran
with is never lost.

## Teams

An agent is on teams exactly the way a person is: through the team's
membership. Nothing on the agent itself names a team, so an agent can be on
as many teams as a person can, or none.

| To | Where | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add it to teams as you make it | **Add to teams**, on the drafted card or the full form | Owners for any team; a team's maintainers for theirs |
| Add it to a team, or take it off | Its **Profile** tab or its People profile, under **Teams**: **Add to team** and **Remove** | Owners, and the team's maintainers |
| The same, from the team | The team's **Members** page, under **Agents** (see [agents on a team](/guides/teams/#agents-on-a-team)) | Owners, and the team's maintainers |

A personal agent is on no team; once an owner
[promotes](#promote-a-personal-agent) it, add it to teams like anyone.

The Agents sidebar groups specialists by the teams they are on, each team
by name: an agent on two teams shows under both, and agents on none show
last, under **Not on a team**. Every turn, an agent is told who is on its
teams, who leads them and who owns what; see
[what agents are told](/guides/people-and-teams/#what-agents-are-told). A
team's [budget](/guides/agent-budgets/) caps what the agents on it spend
together. Hearing a team's mentions and review requests, the way a person
on the team does, comes next. <Soon />

## Job and personality

An agent's **job** decides what it does. Its **personality** decides how it
sounds. They are kept apart on purpose: personality is voice only, and
nothing written there can widen what the agent may do.

| | Job | Personality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers | What is it responsible for? What does good look like? | How does it talk? |
| Example | *Cut releases of acme/web on Thursdays. Check required checks pass. Ask a person before tagging.* | *Terse operator. No emoji. Always say what you are waiting on.* |
| Changes | What it pays attention to, what it asks, when it stops | Tone, length, formality, how it asks questions |
| Never changes | | What it may read, change or spend |

Say you hired Bruno from the **Operations Engineer** template. The same
question, put to him with two different personalities:

<Conversation title="DM with Bruno" topic="Personality: Crisp">
<Message name="Priya Shah" time="09:15">

Can we release on a Friday?

</Message>
<Message name="Bruno" agent role="Operations Engineer" time="09:15">

We can, but I'd rather not. If something breaks, fewer people are around to fix it. Thursday gives us a day to watch it.

</Message>
</Conversation>

<Conversation title="DM with Bruno" topic="Personality: Terse operator">
<Message name="Priya Shah" time="09:15">

Can we release on a Friday?

</Message>
<Message name="Bruno" agent role="Operations Engineer" time="09:15">

Possible. Not advised: thin weekend cover. Prefer Thursday.

</Message>
</Conversation>

### Personality presets

| Preset | Voice |
| --- | --- |
| **Crisp** (default) | Clear and direct, short sentences, no filler. Warm but businesslike. |
| **Friendly** | Warm and encouraging, plain words, the occasional light touch. Still to the point. |
| **Socratic** | Helps people think. Asks a good question when it moves things forward, then gives a clear view. |
| **Terse operator** | As few words as the job needs. Facts, status, next step. No pleasantries. |

Add free text to refine the preset: *Answers in Spanish when asked in
Spanish.* or *Uses British spelling.*

## Its face

Every agent has a face: a small bot, drawn from seven parts, that shows
wherever the agent does: in Chat, on its profile, in the People directory,
as an assignee or a reviewer, on the commits it authors, in notifications.
Nothing is uploaded; the face is drawn on the page, so it is sharp at any
size and the same everywhere.

| Part | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| **Shape** | Round, square, squircle, blob, hex |
| **Colour** | Lavender, mint, peach, sky, pink, lemon, teal, coral, moss, slate. Each reads on the light theme and the dark one. |
| **Eyes** | Dots, round, wide, happy, visor, sleepy |
| **Mouth** | Smile, grin, flat, open, none |
| **Antenna** | None, one, two, bulb |
| **Accessory** | Nothing, headphones, bow, glasses, hat, spark |
| **Pattern** | Plain, stripes, dots, sheen |

An agent you haven't given a face wears the one drawn from its `avatar_seed`
(its handle, unless you set another): the same seed always draws the same
face, so every agent has a face of its own from the moment it's made, and a
rename never changes it. Faces are alive but calm: each blinks every few
seconds at a pace of its own, so a list of them never blinks in unison, and
breathes a little. What the agent is doing shows on it:

| The face | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Blinking, breathing | Idle. |
| Eyes narrowed, a soft pulse | Working on a session or a reply. |
| Eyes shut, a small *z* | Paused or out of budget. |
| A grin and a bounce, briefly | A session finished well. |

If your system asks for reduced motion, faces hold still, in the pose
their state would give them. At 20 px and under, the accessory and
pattern are left off so the face still reads. g1t itself keeps its own
mark, the pixel 1 of the logo, in place of a face. An agent with an
uploaded picture shows that instead.

**The agent marker is always shown.** Whatever the face, every agent's
avatar carries a small bot on a lavender disc in its corner, from 16 px up,
and reads to a screen reader as *Margo, agent*. Below 16 px the squircle
tile the face sits on tells it from a person's round avatar. Nothing you
choose turns the marker off.

### Choose a face

Open the agent's **Profile** (or the card while you
[describe a new one](#describe-it)) and find **Face**:

1. The preview shows the face large, and how it looks idle, working,
   asleep and happy.
2. Choose any part from its row: each choice is drawn as the face would
   look with it.
3. **Shuffle** draws a face at random. **Reset to its own** gives the agent
   back the face its seed draws.
4. **Match its voice** borrows the expression its personality preset
   suggests (a terse operator gets a visor and a flat mouth, a friendly one
   happy eyes and a smile). Only when you choose it: a preset never changes
   a face behind your back.
5. **Save changes** (or **Create**). The face is part of the agent's
   definition, so it is a saved version like any other change.

Owners change a workspace agent's face; a member changes their own
personal agent's. In the API, the face is `look` on the agent, an object
with the seven parts, or `null` for the seed's face. Set it through the
same update call as any other field, and read it back wherever the agent
is returned.

## Subagents

Subagents are the specialised help an agent keeps for its own work, the
way a person keeps tools for parts of a job. An agent hired from the **QA
Engineer** template comes with two:

| Subagent | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `flake-hunter` | Runs a flaky test repeatedly, narrows down when and why it fails, and reports the cause with evidence. |
| `migration-checker` | Checks a database migration for locking, data loss, irreversible steps and missing indexes. |

See, add and change an agent's subagents on its profile. They run inside
the agent's [sessions](/guides/agent-sessions/), each as a child session,
and these rules hold:

- **Defined on the agent.** Each has a name such as `flake-hunter`, one
  line on what it is for, its own instructions, and its own model limits,
  which always sit inside its agent's.
- **Not members.** They never appear in Chat or member lists, and never
  talk to people. They report to their agent, which speaks for them.
- **Never wider than their agent.** Their access, budget and audience are
  the agent's or narrower. What they spend is paid by the session that
  started them, within its cap.
- **Several at once.** A session runs up to 4 children at once, and never
  more of one subagent than its own limit (1 to 8). The session page shows
  each in its tree.

## Back office and front office

Every agent is **back office**: it works with your team and never talks to
anyone outside the company.

An agent hired from the **Sales Operations** template, David by default,
shows how much a back-office agent can do without ever contacting a
customer. He reads the customer conversations
the workspace already has, and:

- summarizes what's happening per account and across them: who is at
  risk, what keeps being asked for, and what was promised;
- posts a weekly voice-of-the-customer digest;
- prepares account notes before a call;
- links feature requests to the accounts asking for them, so Product sees
  the demand.

Customer-data rules apply to everything he reads. See
[what agents can do for whom](/guides/agent-access/).

**Front office** agents, which talk to customers directly by email, a
support widget or a shared channel, come later. They will need stricter
rails: an owner switch per agent, only public and approved Docs content,
a person's approval for anything that promises, refunds or commits, and a
clear "you're talking to an agent" label. Until then, a workspace can't
set an agent to face customers.

## Agents know each other

Every agent, not only g1t, knows the team: each colleague's name, title,
the teams it is on, responsibilities and status. It also knows the people and agents on
its own [teams](/guides/teams/): who leads each, what each person owns,
who reports to whom, who is around now and their local time, and who to
ask when a person is needed. It is told this every turn; see
[what agents are told](/guides/people-and-teams/#what-agents-are-told).
When a question belongs to someone else, it makes one of three moves,
always in the open.

| Move | What happens | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Consult** | It asks the colleague a quick question and brings the answer back. You stay with the agent you asked, and the colleague does no work in the conversation. The exchange shows as a card in the thread. | *David asked Margo* |
| **Hand off** | It gives the colleague the work, with a brief. A specialist offers first and hands off when you say yes; g1t hands off when a specialist's role fits. See [Hand off](#hand-off). | *"That's Margo's area. Want me to hand it to her?"* |
| **Steer** | When you are about to do something another role owns, it says so and names who to check with. | *"We're in the release freeze. Check with Bruno before merging."* |

### Who is in the conversation

Every time an agent answers, it is told where it is: a direct message, a
group message, or a public or private channel and its name, and who is in
it. People and agents are listed by name and handle, with each agent's
title. In a large channel every agent is listed, and people up to 20,
then a count.

That tells it two things:

- **Who reads its answer.** Only the members of the conversation.
  Writing the name or `@handle` of anyone else reaches no one, so an
  agent's mention of someone who isn't a member shows as a plain name,
  with no link and no notification.
- **That a mention hands nothing over.** An agent's messages never wake
  another agent, even with an `@mention`. Only a hand-off does. An agent
  never says it asked, told or handed work to someone unless it did.

### Hand off

A hand-off goes where the colleague can see it:

| Where you asked | Where the work goes |
| --- | --- |
| A channel or group message the colleague is in | The brief is posted right there, addressed to them, and they answer in the same place. |
| Anywhere else, such as your direct message with g1t | A group message opens with you, the agent that handed off and the colleague. The brief is posted there, and a card where you asked links to it. The next hand-off between the same three reuses that group message. |

The colleague works for you, with your access: in the group message, it
can draw only on what you three may all see. Its answer is charged to its
own budget, like any reply, and the hand-off counts as a hop. An agent
can't hand work to itself, to `@g1t`, to an agent that has already handled
the request, or to one that is paused or out of budget; it says so
instead.

<Conversation title="g1t" topic="A hand-off from your direct message with g1t">
<Message name="Chase Pierce" time="09:12">

How do I hire an engineering manager?

</Message>
<Message name="g1t" agent role="Orchestrator" time="09:12">

Mike, our technical recruiter, owns hiring. I've handed it to him in a group message with you and me, where he'll start on the role brief.

</Message>
</Conversation>

In a group message with several agents, a message that mentions some of
them goes only to those; one that mentions none goes to all of them.

In a workspace that has hired Sam from **Support Specialist** and Margo
from **QA Engineer**:

<Conversation title="# web" topic="A consult, as it will show">
<Message name="Dana Ruiz" time="11:02">

@sam a customer says exports skip archived rows. Is that expected?

</Message>
<Message name="Sam" agent role="Support Specialist" time="11:03">

It's expected: archived rows are left out unless the customer turns on **Include archived** before exporting. I checked with Margo, who confirmed it's covered by the export tests.

*Sam asked Margo · 2 messages*

</Message>
</Conversation>

The same rails hold along every chain:

- **The audience.** A colleague can only contribute what the
  conversation's audience may see.
- **The asker's access.** Nobody gets more done through a chain of agents
  than they could do themselves.
- **The bill.** A consult is charged to the reply that asked. A
  hand-off's work is charged to the colleague's own budget.
- **No ping-pong.** An agent can't send work back to an agent that has
  already handled it, in the same chain, without a person stepping in.
- **The hop limit.** A chain stops after six hops and hands back to a
  person.

## Talk to an agent

There are two ways to reach an agent in [Chat](/guides/chat/):

- **DM it.** In a direct message, it answers every message you send. In a
  group message with several agents, mention the ones you want; a message
  that mentions none of them goes to all of them.
- **Mention it** in a channel it is a member of: `@margo …`. In a channel,
  an agent answers only when it is mentioned, and replies in the thread.

Agents reach each other only by [handing off](#hand-off), never by
mentioning each other. Each hand-off is a *hop*, and a chain started by
one person's message stops after six hops, so agents can't keep each other
busy without a person.

While it writes, the agent shows as typing. Its answer is charged to its
own budget; see [what an agent costs](#what-an-agent-costs).

<Aside type="note" title="What a reply can see">
A reply reads the conversation it is in: the thread, or the latest 30
messages of the channel or DM. It knows who asked and whether they can
change code, and it is given the facts it [remembers](/guides/agent-memory/)
for that place and the passages of [artifacts](/guides/artifacts/#agents-and-artifacts)
closest to what was asked. While it answers it can look up code, issues,
pull requests with their checks, earlier messages and artifacts, up to 8
lookups a reply, only within what everyone in the conversation can see.
In public channels and conversations of more than 50 people it reads no
code. See [what agents can do for whom](/guides/agent-access/).
</Aside>

## Model routing

Nobody picks a model to get work done. **Auto** sends each step of an
agent's work to the least costly tier that can do it:

| Tier | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| **Fast** | Chat replies, triage, answering questions. |
| **Standard** | Making and revising changes, most reviews. |
| **Most capable** | Planning, very large reviews, and work that failed before. |

Chat replies start on the fast tier. The models behind each tier are
listed under [Auto](/guides/models/#auto), and move to newer models as
providers release them, with nothing for you to change.

An agent's definition does not pick a model. It **limits** Auto:

| Limit | Means | For example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Floor** | Never route below this tier. | A reviewer that must be careful: *never below Standard*. Its chat replies run on Standard too. |
| **Ceiling** | Never route above this tier. | A cheap triage agent: *never above Standard*. |
| **Providers** | Where its model calls may go: g1t's models, your workspace's own provider, or both. | A support agent restricted to your own provider, so customer conversations never reach g1t's model accounts. |

When a floor and a ceiling disagree, you are asked to fix them before
saving. If an old definition has them crossed, the ceiling wins: it is the
spending limit, and a limit is never crossed.

## Effort

Each agent has an **effort** setting: how hard it works. One setting moves
three things together, so you never tune them apart:

| Effort | Starts on | The model reasons | A session takes at most |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Auto** (the default) | Where the work starts | Medium, and High on a session someone had to steer | 8 steps, 10 once raised |
| **Low** | The fast tier | Lightly | 5 steps |
| **Medium** | Where the work starts: fast for chat replies, standard for sessions | Moderately | 8 steps |
| **High** | Standard or above | Deeply | 10 steps |
| **Max** | The most capable tier | As hard as it can | 12 steps |

The floor and the ceiling still hold: an agent at **Max** with a ceiling of
Standard runs on Standard. How hard the model reasons is sent only to
g1t's tiers whose model takes it; a model your own route names is sent as
it is.

Set it on the agent's **Profile**, under **Models**, or on its **Spend**
tab. Owners change it, and each change is a new version of the agent.
Beside the setting, each level shows what a typical task has cost that
agent, as budgets count it (the model and the
[agent rate](/guides/usage-and-billing/#the-agent-rate)): the median of its
finished sessions at that level over the last 28 days, how many there
were, and how many were accepted (finished with nobody having to step
in). A level it has not run at shows no figure.

Every session and reply records the level it ran at, which is what the
figures and the weekly [suggestions](/guides/spend/#spend-less-keep-quality)
are measured from.

### Your own providers

Connect a model provider, or any compatible endpoint, in the workspace's
**Integrations**, under **AI models**. See [model providers](/guides/models/#connect-a-provider).
Then, on an agent:

- **Both** (the default, when nothing is chosen): the agent may use
  whatever the workspace allows.
- **Only your own provider**: every model call goes to your account. Your
  provider bills you for the model, and g1t charges only the agent rate.
  The agent never touches g1t's models.
- **Only g1t's models**: the agent never uses your keys.

For own endpoints, an advanced setting **pins** one model, written
`provider/model`. Pinning replaces the tier's model and is not shown by
default; most agents should leave it empty.

Every reply records which model ran it.

## Budgets

An agent's budget is checked before every reply and every session step. A
cap left empty means no cap of its own, and only the workspace's limits
apply. For how the workspace's agent budget, each agent's budget and each
session's cap fit together, see
[agent budgets and spend](/guides/agent-budgets/).

| Cap | Resets | What happens when it is reached |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monthly** | On the 1st, UTC | The agent stops taking new work and says so in the thread: *I'm out of budget for October. An owner can raise my monthly limit on my profile.* |
| **Daily** | At midnight UTC | The agent says it has used today's budget and will be back tomorrow. |
| **Per session** | Each session | A session starts with this cap when it is lower than the workspace's session cap. At the cap it waits for an owner to [approve more](/guides/agent-sessions/#caps-and-approval). |

Above the agent's own caps, the workspace's limits still hold:

1. The workspace's [spend limit](/guides/usage-and-billing/#your-spend-limit)
   and its [AI credit](/guides/usage-and-billing/#ai-credit).
2. The workspace's [agent budget](/guides/agent-budgets/#the-agent-budget),
   across every agent.
3. The agent's monthly and daily caps.
4. The session's cap.
5. The plan's [per-run caps](/guides/usage-and-billing/#caps).

The first one used up stops the work. The agent's page shows
its spend this month against its monthly cap, and its status turns to
**Out of budget** when a cap stops it.

## What an agent costs

An agent costs nothing until someone talks to it or gives it work. Each
reply is charged to the workspace, against that agent's budget:

| On | You pay |
| --- | --- |
| g1t's models | The model provider's price for the tokens, with no markup, plus the [g1t agent rate](/guides/usage-and-billing/#the-agent-rate) on the same tokens. |
| Your own provider | Your provider bills you for the model. g1t charges the agent rate for your own model key only. |
| Work in a sandbox | The above, plus [sandbox time](/guides/usage-and-billing/#sandbox-time) at cost plus 20%. |

The live agent rate is on [g1t.sh/pricing](https://g1t.sh/pricing). Its
[effort](#effort) decides most of what a task costs: the tier it starts on,
how much the model reasons and how many steps a session may take.

### A worked example

Say you hired Margo from the **QA Engineer** template. She answers *what
should we test before Thursday?* in a thread of a dozen
messages. The reply reads about 3,000 tokens and writes about 300, on the
fast tier. Say the fast model costs $1 per million input tokens and $5 per
million output tokens, and the agent rate is $0.25 per million tokens
(these are example numbers; the real ones are on the pricing page):

| Line | Calculation | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model, input | 3,000 × $1 / 1,000,000 | $0.0030 |
| Model, output | 300 × $5 / 1,000,000 | $0.0015 |
| Agent rate | 3,300 × $0.25 / 1,000,000 | $0.0008 |
| **The reply** | | **about $0.005** |

At that size, a $40 monthly budget pays for about 8,000 replies. On your own
provider, the same reply costs $0.0008 at g1t, and your provider bills the
model.

## On issues and pull requests

An agent comments on issues and pull requests, and reviews pull requests,
as itself: its face, its name with an **Agent** badge, and **on behalf of
@person**, the person it was working for. It never does more there than
that person could: they must be able to read the repository and comment
on it.

Its reviews are **advisory**. The verdict shows, marked **Advisory**, but
it never counts toward the approvals a branch requires or a code owner's
approval, and a request for changes from it never blocks a merge. A person
still approves. An agent doesn't review drafts, and writes at most 5
comments and reviews on one issue or pull request an hour. See
[agent reviews](/guides/pull-requests/#agent-reviews).

## What it may do alone

Each agent says what it may do by itself and what needs a person first.
These apply when the agent works on code and docs; rules, protected
branches and required checks still apply on top.

| Action | Choices | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open pull requests | Alone, or after approval | Alone |
| Merge | Alone, after approval, or never | After approval |
| Deploy to production | After approval, or never | After approval |
| Edit docs | Alone, or as a suggestion | As a suggestion |

These four are also on the agent's **Abilities** tab, beside everything
else it may do: each connected integration's actions, MCP servers an owner
adds, and whose connection each runs on, with four levels (alone, alone
when asked for it, ask first, never). See
[agent abilities](/guides/agent-abilities/).

An agent also never does more for someone than that person could do
themselves. See [what agents can do for whom](/guides/agent-access/).

## Its computer

Every workspace agent has a computer of its own: a Linux machine on g1t
cloud with git, Node, Python, Go, Rust, Java, .NET and Ruby, and a home,
`/home/agent`, that stays between sessions. Clones, installed tools, build
caches and notes are there the next time. It is the agent's alone; no two
agents share one, and no person signs in to it.

It is a persistent home, not an always-on machine:

- **Sessions use it; chat replies never do.** A [session](/guides/agent-sessions/)
  runs commands with `run_command` and reads and writes files with
  `computer_read_file` and `computer_write_file`, as its
  [abilities](/guides/agent-abilities/#its-computer) allow. Each session
  works in its own directory, `/home/agent/sessions/<session id>`, in the
  shared home. A quick answer in chat never wakes a machine.
- **It sleeps when idle.** Ten minutes after the last command with nothing
  running, its home is saved as it was and the machine stops. The next
  command wakes it and puts the home back, which takes a few seconds. A
  command that was running at the time is never cut off for idleness.
- **Its home holds 5 GB.** That is included with the agent, with no charge
  for the disk. It is a hard cap: a home over it cannot be saved, the
  Computer tab says which directories are largest, and the agent is asked
  to trim them. After an hour over the cap, the machine stops without
  saving.
- **It costs machine time while awake.** Awake time is
  [sandbox time](/guides/usage-and-billing/#sandbox-time), at cost plus
  20%, under the agent and whoever asked for the session that woke it.
  Asleep it costs nothing. Waking it is admitted the way an agent run is:
  a paused workspace, or one past its limit, is refused and the agent
  says so in its report.
- **Reset wipes the home.** The agent's memory and artifacts are kept;
  its files, clones and command history go.
- **Archiving the agent** puts the computer to sleep and keeps its disk
  for 30 days, then deletes it.

Every command runs as the agent in `bash`, with the output returned when it
ends and a timeout of 120 seconds unless the agent asks for more (up to 30
minutes). The output is kept with the session and on the Computer tab, cut
at 64 KB. Nothing waits for input, and nothing keeps running once the
command ends. The agent cannot push to a repository from its computer; to
change code it drafts an issue, as before.

It runs on g1t cloud. Pinning an agent's computer to one of your own
[runners](/guides/self-hosted-runners/), a browser of its own, web search
and taking over its terminal are coming.

## The agent's page

Each agent has a page at `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/agents/<handle>`:

| Tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Sessions** | Every session it is working on, then every one it worked on before, each child under the session that started it. See [sessions](/guides/agent-sessions/). |
| **Skills** | The [skills](/guides/agent-skills/) it has: g1t's foundational ones and your library's, each on or off. |
| **Abilities** | What it may do, in groups, with a level for each: alone, alone when asked for it, ask first, or never; whose connection an integration runs on; MCP servers owners add. See [agent abilities](/guides/agent-abilities/). |
| **Computer** | [Its computer](#its-computer): asleep or awake and since when, its disk against the 5 GB it holds, where it runs, and the commands it ran most recently with their output. Owners (or the member, for a personal agent) wake it, put it to sleep, or reset it. |
| **Memory** | What it remembers, by scope, with where each fact came from. Pin, correct or forget facts. See [agent memory](/guides/agent-memory/). |
| **Routines** | Work it does on a schedule or when something happens. See [routines](/guides/agent-routines/). |
| **Spend** | Its [effort](#effort) and what each level has cost it, suggestions to spend less, and spend this month against its budget: by day, kind of work, model and who asked, and its costliest sessions. |
| **Activity** | Its latest replies and sessions, with who asked, the model and the cost. |
| **Profile** | Its definition: identity, [face](#its-face), job, personality, models and effort, budget and what it may do alone, with every saved version. Owners edit a workspace agent here, and a member their own personal agent, in the form or [in words](#change-it-in-words). |

The **Agents** page above them all shows the agent budget, sessions waiting
on you and working now, every agent with its live sessions and its month's
spend, where the month went, upcoming routines and recently finished
sessions.

Its status is one of **Idle**, **Working**, **Waiting on you**, **Out of
budget** or **Paused**, on the Agents page and beside its name in Chat.

To retire an agent, an owner **archives** it from its profile; a member can
archive their own personal agent. It stops answering, leaves the member
lists, and its history stays.
