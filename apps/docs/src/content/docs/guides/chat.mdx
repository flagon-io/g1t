**Chat is where a workspace talks.** Everyone in a workspace can find its
channels, write in the same threads and message each other directly. You
can ask a teammate a question in a channel or a direct message, and the
answer arrives in the place you asked.

Chat is open to every member of a workspace, on every plan, the free one
too. People outside it can talk in its [public channels](#public-channels)
and in the channels they are [asked into as guests](#guests). Chat is never
charged: no seats, no per-message price and no history cutoff.

Open **Chat** in the rail on the left, or go to
`g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/chat`.

## A conversation

Here is a morning in `#web`, a channel for the web app, its releases and
its bugs:

<Conversation title="# web" topic="The web app, its releases and its bugs">
<Message name="Priya Shah" time="10:42">

Thursday's release is #412, #415 and #417. @dana can you check the invite emails in #412 before then? They change what customers receive.

</Message>
<Message name="Dana Brooks" time="10:43">

On it. I'll reply in a thread here with anything I find.

</Message>
<Message name="Ana Ruiz" time="10:45">

#417 is merged. Charts read correctly in dark mode now.

</Message>
</Conversation>

Priya **mentioned** `@dana`, so the message is counted as a mention in
Dana's sidebar and, with the default settings, notifies her.

Everyone is shown by name. A person is their display name, or their
username as they wrote it when they have none. Their username is on their
card: select a name or avatar to see it. Messages someone sends within
five minutes of each other sit under one name, and each new day starts with a divider.
The sidebar, the people typing, notifications and phone alerts all name
people the same way.

Messages that agents wrote in a workspace's chat in the past still show
where they were posted, under the name the agent had, or as a deleted
agent.

## Channels

A channel is a named conversation, such as `#web`, `#releases` or
`#support`. Every workspace starts with `#general`, and everyone new is put
in it the first time they open Chat (owners can choose other
[default channels](#workspace-chat-settings)).

Each channel is one of three kinds, and its mark says which, in the
sidebar, in Browse channels and at the top of the channel:

| | Workspace `#` | Private 🔒 | Public 🌐 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who can find it | Every member, under **Browse channels** | Only the people in it | Every member, and anyone on g1t with its link |
| Who can join | Any member | Only people someone in it adds, or lets in on a [request](#ask-to-join) | Anyone signed in to g1t with a confirmed email address, except someone outside the workspace who was [taken out of it](#public-channels) |
| Who can read it | Every member | Only the people in it | Every member, and anyone signed in to g1t with a confirmed email address |
| Who can make one | Members, unless owners keep it to themselves | Members, unless owners keep it to themselves | Workspace owners |

Anyone in a channel who is not a member of the workspace shows as a
**Guest**, beside their name on every message, in the channel's people and
in its details, so who is in the workspace is never in doubt.

### Create a channel

<Steps>

1. In Chat's sidebar, choose **Create a channel**.
2. Give it a **name**. Names are what people type after `#`: up to 80
   lowercase letters, digits, dashes and underscores. `Web Team` becomes
   `web-team`.
3. Optionally, say what the channel is for. That line shows under its name.
4. Under **Who can open it**, choose **Workspace**, **Private**, or (for
   workspace owners) **Public**.
5. Choose **Create channel**. You are its owner and first member. Invite
   people once it is made.

</Steps>

A workspace's owners can keep creating workspace channels, private channels
or both to themselves ([chat settings](#workspace-chat-settings)). When they
have, **Create a channel** is turned off for everyone else and says why, and
a kind someone may not create is shown turned off, with why. Only owners
make a channel public.

### Public channels

A public channel is open to anyone signed in to g1t with a confirmed email
address: a community, a support desk, an open project's chat. Share its
link, `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/chat/<channel>`; anyone who opens it reads it,
and **Join** (or simply writing in it) puts it in their sidebar.

- People outside the workspace talk there as **guests**. They see that
  channel and the workspace's other public ones, and nothing else of the
  workspace: no other channels, no direct messages, no people directory.
- Only people come into another workspace's channels: not a workspace's
  tokens, and not agents. Someone whose email address isn't
  confirmed yet is asked to confirm it first, and someone signed out is
  asked to sign in.
- **@g1t works for the workspace's members only.** Guests never start a
  conversation with g1t, in a public channel or anywhere else, so nobody
  outside the workspace spends its AI.
- Only a workspace owner makes a channel public, when creating it or later
  from its details: under **Visibility**, turn on **Open to anyone on g1t**.
  The switch is there for workspace and public channels that aren't
  archived; a private channel stays private. Turning it off keeps the channel to the workspace again: guests
  who joined on their own leave it, and guests someone asked in stay.
- A channel's owners and the workspace's owners can take anyone out of it
  from its details. Someone outside the workspace taken out of a public
  channel cannot join it again on their own; asking them in again lifts
  that.

### Guests

A guest is someone outside the workspace in one of its channels. Besides
joining a public channel, someone can be asked into any channel, private
ones too, without joining the workspace: a contractor, a customer, a
friend from another team.

<Steps>

1. Open the channel's details and choose **Add**.
2. Type their g1t username and choose **Invite @username as a guest**.
3. They see the invitation in their own Chat sidebar, under
   **Invitations**, and choose **Join** or **Decline**.

</Steps>

A guest's channels show in their own sidebar under **Other workspaces**,
with their unread counts. Opening one shows that workspace's chat as a
guest sees it, with a **Guest** mark at the top and a way back to their
own.

Owners can always ask guests in. Members can too, unless owners turned off
**Members can invite outside collaborators** in the workspace's
[permissions](/guides/workspaces/#member-privileges), the same setting as
for repositories. Guests themselves add no one. Pending invitations show in
the channel's details, where whoever may take people out can take one
back.

### Ask to join

Opening the link of a channel you cannot open (a private one, or a
workspace one when you are not in the workspace) shows **You can't open
#channel** and a way to **Ask to join**, with an optional note of up to
300 characters. The page is
the same whether or not the channel exists, so asking never tells anyone
about a channel they may not see.

The people who could add you see your request in the channel's details,
with a count on its members button, and choose **Let in** or **Decline**.
Anyone in the channel can let in a member of the workspace; someone outside
it is let in, as a guest, by those who may invite guests.

### Join, leave and browse

- **Browse channels** lists every workspace and public channel, and the
  private ones you're in, at `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/chat/browse`, and at the
  end of the sidebar's **Channels**. A guest sees the public ones there. Choose a channel anywhere on its row to read it,
  and choose **Join** to post in it and see it in your sidebar. A private channel
  you're not in never appears.
- **Archived**, on the same page, lists archived channels.
- You can leave a channel, and join a workspace or public one again at any time.
- When you join, everything already said counts as read, so a busy channel
  doesn't greet you with its whole history as unread.

### Rename, archive and change the topic

Open a channel's details (the members button at the top right) to change
it.

| Change | Who can |
| --- | --- |
| **Topic** | Any member of the channel. |
| **Rename** | The channel's owners and the workspace's owners, or only the workspace's owners if they chose so in [chat settings](#workspace-chat-settings). The address follows the new name for everyone who has it open. |
| **Archive** | The same people as rename. An archived channel leaves everyone's sidebar and nobody can post in it, but its history stays readable. |
| **Unarchive** | The same people, from the archived channel itself or **Browse channels → Archived**. |

`#general` is never renamed or archived. Everyone looking at a channel sees
a change to it the moment it is made.

### Star and mute

- **Star** a channel to keep it at the top of your sidebar.
- **Mute** a channel to stop its unread count from drawing your eye. You
  still see mentions of you.

Both are yours alone; nobody else's sidebar changes.

## The sidebar

Chat's sidebar lists, from the top:

| Section | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Pinned** | Conversations you pinned (hover over one and choose the pin, or right-click it). Only when you have some. |
| **Channels** | The channels you're in, by name, then **Browse channels** with how many more you could join. **+** creates one. |
| **Direct messages** | [g1t](#talk-with-g1t) first, then your direct messages, the latest first. The pencil starts a new message. Empty, it says **Message a teammate.** |

Unread conversations are in bold with a count; mentions of you are counted
in lavender. **All**, **Unread** and **Mentions** filter every section, and
**Jump to channel or person** finds any of them by name.

Choose a section's name to fold it. A folded section still shows what's
unread and the conversation you have open, and it stays folded on that
device until you open it again.

## Direct messages

A direct message is a private conversation between you and up to eight
other members. Start one with **New message** in Chat's sidebar and pick
who to message.

- The same group of members always gets the same conversation, whatever
  order you pick them in.
- A DM with only yourself is a place for notes.

## Talk with g1t

**g1t** is at the top of your direct messages, with its mark for a face.
Write to it like anyone else and it answers, with your workspace's own AI
provider and the model an owner chose: see [Ask g1t](/guides/ask/) for
setting it up and what it knows.

- It is the same conversation as **Ask** in the top bar: a question asked
  there is posted here with the page it was asked from, and the answer
  shows in both places as it is written.
- The answer streams in as g1t writes it. **Stop answering**, over the box
  you write in, ends it; what came stays. Writing again while it answers
  does the same, and g1t answers your newest words.
- **New conversation**, at the top, leaves a mark: g1t reads only what is
  said after it. Everything before stays to read.
- **⋯ → Clear conversation…**, at the top, or `/clear` typed in the box,
  deletes everything in it for good, after asking: see
  [Clear the conversation](/guides/ask/#clear-the-conversation).
- Under its latest answer, **Answer again** has it answer anew, and
  **Copy** copies an answer. Each answer says which provider and model
  wrote it.
- Until an owner chooses a provider, g1t replies with where to choose one.
- Only you and g1t are in it: g1t can't be added to a group message or a
  channel, and mentioning `@g1t` in a channel doesn't ask it anything.
  You can delete g1t's messages in it, as well as your own.
- It notifies you of an answer the way any direct message does, once the
  answer is done.

## Threads

Reply to any message to start a thread under it. The thread keeps a side
conversation out of the channel, and the message shows how many replies it
has and when the last one came.

The **⋯** on a message (hover over it; on a phone, press and hold it) and
the **⋯** at the top of an open thread have **Copy link to thread**: a link
that opens the conversation with the thread beside it, to paste anywhere in
g1t.

## Mentions

Type `@` and a name to mention a person by username: `@priya`, `@dana`.
Suggestions match display names too, so `@Pri` finds Priya Nair. In the
conversation the mention reads as the name people know, `@Priya Nair`, and
opens their card; the message itself keeps `@priya`. A mention is counted
in that person's sidebar separately from other unread messages, so it
stands out.

An address such as `me@example.com` is never read as a mention.

## Format a message

Messages can have bold, italic and struck-through words, links, inline
code, code blocks, lists and quotes. Format as you write, three ways:

- **The formatting bar** above the text. **T** at the bottom left of the
  composer shows or hides it, and your choice is kept on that device. On a
  phone it starts hidden; when it is shown, swipe it sideways for the rest.
- **Shortcuts.** Select words, or start typing after one. The keyboard
  button at the end of the bar lists them too.
- **Markdown, typed inline.** It turns into its formatting as you type:
  `*bold*` becomes **bold** when you type the closing `*`.

| Formatting | Shortcut (⌘ on a Mac) | Or type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bold | Ctrl+B | `*bold*` or `**bold**` |
| Italic | Ctrl+I | `_italic_` |
| Strikethrough | Ctrl+Shift+X | `~struck~` or `~~struck~~` |
| Link | Ctrl+K | Select words and paste an address over them |
| Inline code | Ctrl+Shift+C or Ctrl+E | `` `code` `` |
| Code block | Ctrl+Alt+Shift+C | ` ``` ` then Enter, or ` ```ts ` to name the language |
| Bulleted list | Ctrl+Shift+8 | `- ` at the start of a line |
| Numbered list | Ctrl+Shift+7 | `1. ` at the start of a line |
| Quote | Ctrl+Shift+9 | `> ` at the start of a line |

**Ctrl+K** asks where the link goes, and for its text when nothing is
selected. On a link already, it changes the address or removes the link.
A link goes to a web address, an email address or a page on g1t; anything
else is refused. Undo (**Ctrl+Z**) takes formatting back off.

### Enter and new lines

| Key | In a line of text | In a list | In a code block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Enter** | Sends | Adds an item; on an empty item, leaves the list | Adds a line; three in a row leave the block |
| **Shift+Enter** | Starts a new line | Adds an item | Adds a line |
| **Ctrl+Enter** | Sends | Sends | Sends |

In a quote, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts the next line of the quote.

### What a message shows

Every message is shown with its formatting.
Code blocks with a language g1t knows (`ts`, `rust`, `python`, `sh`,
`sql`, `json` and others) are coloured, and have a copy button. A web
address on its own becomes a link, and links open in a new tab. A heading
shows as a bold line, and a rule as a line across.

A table written in Markdown shows as a table: a header row, a row of
`---` under it (`:--`, `:-:` or `--:` to line a column up left, centre
or right), then a row per line. A wide table scrolls sideways inside the
message. The composer has no table to edit, so editing a message with
one shows its rows as the lines they were written as.

```md
| Session | Length | Who |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Welcome and tour | 45 min | Ana |
| Your first issue | 60 min | Margo |
```

Chat never shows HTML: `<b>` reads as typed. An image written in Markdown
shows as a link to it, not the image.

### Messages are Markdown

What you send is kept as Markdown, the formatting written as `**bold**`,
`_italic_`, `~~struck~~` and so on. That is what you edit when you change
a message, so formatting survives the round trip. Text you paste without formatting, such as from a terminal, is
read as Markdown too; paste with **Ctrl+Shift+V** to keep it exactly as it
is. Notifications, pop-ups and browser notifications show a message's
words without the marks: `**Release** is _done_` reads *Release is done*.

To show a character that would format, put a backslash before it: `\*`
shows `*`.

## Live

Chat is live. Messages, edits and deletions appear for everyone in the
channel the moment they happen, without reloading. You also see:

- **Typing.** Who is writing right now, in the channel or in a thread.
- **Channel changes.** A new name, topic, or archiving.
- **Read state.** Your sidebar shows unread counts per channel, and the
  channel scrolls to the first message you haven't read.

If your connection drops, Chat reconnects on its own and fills in what you
missed.

## Edit and delete

You can edit or delete your own messages. On a computer, hover your message,
open its **⋯** and choose **Edit message**: it opens in the composer, in
place, with its formatting. Enter or **Save** keeps the change, Esc or
**Cancel** leaves it as it was. On a phone, press and hold the message and
choose **Edit**. An edited message says so. A deleted message is removed
for everyone; replies under it stay.

A message can be up to 40,000 characters, and is written in Markdown (see
[format a message](#format-a-message)).

## Reactions

A reaction says *seen*, *agreed* or *done* without another message.

| Where you are | How to react |
| --- | --- |
| On a computer | Hover a message and choose **React** in the toolbar that appears, then pick an emoji. |
| On a phone | Long-press a message, then pick one of the quick reactions in the sheet, or open the picker. |
| On an existing reaction | Click its pill to add yours. Click it again to take yours back. |

The picker has a search box, **Recently used**, the standard emoji by group,
a skin tone, and a **This workspace** tab for your
[custom emoji](#custom-emoji). Arrow keys move through it and Enter picks.

Hover a reaction's pill to see who reacted, such as *Priya, Dana and 3
more*. Your own reactions are highlighted.

| Limit | |
| --- | --- |
| Different emoji on one message | 50. More of an emoji already there always fits. |
| What you can react with | One emoji: a standard one, or one of the workspace's own, written `:name:`. |

## Custom emoji

A workspace can add its own emoji: the team's logo, a mascot, an inside
joke. Everyone in the workspace can use them in messages and reactions.

### Add an emoji

<Steps>

1. Open the workspace's **Emoji** page: `g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/emoji`.
2. Under **Add an emoji**, choose an image. It shows in a preview at the
   size it appears in messages.
3. Give it a **name**, such as `shipit`. People type it as `:shipit:`.
4. Choose **Add emoji**.

</Steps>

| | Rules |
| --- | --- |
| File | A PNG, GIF or WebP, up to 256 KB and 512×512 pixels. Animated GIFs and WebPs stay animated. The file's type is read from its contents, not its name. |
| Name | 2 to 32 characters: lowercase letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `+`. Unique in the workspace, and never the name of a standard emoji, such as `:thumbsup:`. |

Custom emoji images are served from `g1tusercontent.com`, apart from the
site, like avatars.

### Aliases

An **alias** is another name for an emoji you already have: both show the
same image. Under **Add an alias**, pick the emoji and type the new name.
Aliases follow the same name rules.

### Who can add emoji

Owners choose in the workspace's [chat settings](#workspace-chat-settings):

| Setting | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Any member** (the default) | Anyone in the workspace can add emoji and aliases. |
| **Owners only** | Members use the workspace's emoji; only owners add them. |

### Remove an emoji

Whoever added an emoji, and the workspace's owners, can remove it with
**Remove** beside it. Removing an emoji removes its aliases too.

### Use one

Type `:` and a couple of letters in the composer, and a list of matching
emoji appears, the workspace's own marked **This workspace**. Up and Down
move through it, Enter or Tab puts the emoji in, and Escape closes it. A whole
`:shortcode:` you type yourself works too.

## Workspace chat settings

A workspace's owners decide what members can do in its chat, under
**Workspace → Chat → Chat settings** (`g1t.sh/<workspace>/-/settings/chat`).
Every member can open the page to see what is allowed; only owners can
change it. g1t enforces each setting, whichever app or API the request
comes from.

| Setting | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| **Creating channels** | Who can create workspace and private channels (**Any member**, the default, or **Owners only**) is set with the workspace's other permissions, under [Permissions → Chat](/guides/permissions/#chat); this page says what is allowed today and leads there. |
| **Renaming and archiving** | **Channel owners and workspace owners** (the default) or **Workspace owners only**. Any member of a channel can still change its topic. |
| **Custom emoji** | Who can add them: **Any member** (the default) or **Owners only**. |
| **Default channels** | The workspace and public channels someone new is put in the first time they open Chat. `#general` unless owners choose others. People already in the workspace are not moved, and anyone can leave a default channel. |

Changing a setting doesn't change what already exists: channels already
made stay, whoever made them.

## Presence and status

Everyone you share a workspace with can see whether you're here, and what
you've said about yourself, wherever your name shows: beside a direct
message in the sidebar, on the card over your name, in a channel's member
list, in the workspace's [People directory](/guides/people-and-teams/#the-directory) and after your name on your messages.
It all moves live; nobody has to reload.

| The dot on an avatar | It means |
| --- | --- |
| Green | **Active**: g1t is open and they've used it in the last 10 minutes. |
| A ring | **Away**: every tab they have open has sat untouched for 10 minutes, or they set themselves away. |
| Amber, with a bar | **Notifications paused**: they're here, but nothing pops up for them until the time they chose. |
| None | **Offline**: no tab of g1t is open. |

### Set a status

Open the menu on your avatar at the foot of the rail (on a phone, **More**,
then **You and help**) and choose **Set a status**. Give it an
emoji and a few words, or pick one:

| | Clears after |
| --- | --- |
| 🗓️ In a meeting | 1 hour |
| 🚌 Commuting | 30 minutes |
| 🎯 Focusing | 1 hour |
| 🤒 Out sick | Today |
| 🌴 On vacation | Never: it stays until you change it |

**Clear after** can be 30 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours, today (your midnight),
this week (the end of your Sunday), never, or a time you choose. When the
time comes your status goes, for everyone at once, whether or not you're
online. Clear it sooner with **Clear status** in the same menu.

### Away

Your dot turns to a ring by itself after 10 minutes without using g1t, and
back to green as soon as you do. **Set yourself away** keeps you away
however much you use it, until you choose **Set yourself active**.

### Statuses from other apps

A status has a source: you, a calendar, or another integration. Calendar
and integration statuses (such as "In a meeting" while one is on your
calendar) are coming with those integrations, which you will connect under
[your integrations](/guides/integrations/#workspace-and-personal). One you set yourself always
wins: a calendar never replaces or clears it. <Soon />

## Notifications

g1t tells you when something is for you, wherever you are in the app.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Pop-ups** | While g1t is open in any tab, a message for you pops up in the corner, whichever page you are on: a DM, a mention, a reply in your thread, a review requested. Reply from the pop-up without leaving the page, or open the conversation. |
| **Sounds** | A short sound when something is for you: a message in a conversation you have open but are not looking at, a direct message, a mention. Which, and how loud, is yours to set; see [Sounds and do not disturb](#sounds-and-do-not-disturb). |
| **Counts** | The rail's Chat badge and each conversation's unread and mention counts update live, in every open tab. |
| **Browser notifications** | Turn them on, and this browser shows a notification when you're away from g1t, even with every tab closed. g1t never pushes to a browser where you're already looking at g1t. |

### Choose what you hear about

Open **Settings → Notifications** (`g1t.sh/settings/notifications`):

| Notify me about | You hear about |
| --- | --- |
| **DMs and mentions** (the default) | Direct messages, mentions and replies in your threads. |
| **Everything** | Every message in conversations you're in, and everything in your notifications. |
| **Nothing** | No pop-ups or browser notifications. Counts still update. |

Under **For a workspace**, you can give one workspace a different level,
such as **Everything** for a small team and **DMs and mentions** for a big
one. **Send a test notification** shows you what one looks like.

### Sounds and do not disturb

Chat makes a sound when something is for you, and only then. Each sound is
short (under a quarter of a second), warm and two-toned, made by g1t
itself: nothing is downloaded. What plays when:

| Sound | When |
| --- | --- |
| **New message** | A message in a conversation you have open, while the window is behind another or you are in another conversation. |
| **Direct message** | A direct message from someone else. |
| **Mention** | A message that @mentions you. It wins over the other two. |
| **Sent** | A soft tick as your message goes. Off until you turn it on. |

The rules behind them:

- Nothing sounds for the conversation you are looking at in a window that
  is in front: what is on screen is silent.
- Nothing sounds for your own messages, in a conversation you
  [muted](#star-and-mute), or while Do not disturb is on.
- At most one sound every 1.5 seconds: a burst of messages is one sound,
  and the same message never sounds twice.
- Browsers allow sound only after you have clicked or typed on the page,
  so the first message after a page opens may be silent.

Open **Settings → Notifications** (`g1t.sh/settings/notifications`),
under **Sounds & notifications**, to change them. The settings are kept
with your account, so every device you sign in on sounds the same:

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sounds** | Whether anything plays at all. Counts and pop-ups still show. | On |
| **Sound set** | **Soft** (warm and low) or **Bright** (higher and shorter). Press play by any sound to hear it. | Soft |
| **Volume** | From 0 to 100. | 60 |
| **New message**, **Direct message**, **Mention**, **Sent** | Each sound on or off. | All on but Sent |
| **Do not disturb** | Off, or for 30 minutes, an hour, two hours, or until tomorrow at 9:00 your time. | Off |
| **Desktop notifications** | While this window is behind another, a message to you shows as a system notification from the open tab. Turning it on asks the browser's permission once, from this switch and never when a page loads. Not offered where browser notifications above are on: those already show it. In [the desktop app](/guides/desktop/) it is on, and the app shows them itself. | Off |

**Do not disturb** is one thing everywhere: the bell-off button at the top
of the chat sidebar, **Pause notifications** on the menu on your avatar,
and the setting above all set it. While it holds, nothing sounds, nothing
pops up and no browser notification is sent, whatever your settings;
counts and your notifications still update, the bell in the top bar shows
a small amber dot, and everyone sees your amber dot. It ends on its own at
the time you chose; **Resume notifications**, or **Off**, ends it early.

At most three pop-ups show at once. Each stays six seconds, and stays while your pointer or keyboard is on
it. Nothing pops up for the conversation you have open.

g1t never asks to show browser notifications when a page loads. After
your first pop-up for a DM or a mention, it offers once: **Get notified
when someone messages you**. If you say no, it doesn't ask again; turn
them on later under **Settings → Notifications**.

[Notifications](/guides/notifications/) are separate: they keep every item until you
deal with it, whatever these settings say.
